    How Zepbound Treatment Works in Peoria, AZ

    How Zepbound Works in Peoria

    Tirzepatide acts on two gut hormone receptors. By activating both the GIP and GLP-1 receptors, it slows stomach emptying, signals fullness to the brain and helps the body manage blood sugar after eating. Many notice fewer cravings and smaller portions. It is injected under the skin of the stomach, thigh or upper arm once a week.

    Who Zepbound Is For

    Zepbound is approved for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. It is also approved for adults with a BMI of 27 or higher who have a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, type 2 diabetes or sleep apnea. A separate approval covers moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. It is not approved for children.

    Zepbound Dosing Schedule in Peoria

    Everyone starts at 2.5 mg. The 2.5 mg dose is a starter dose meant to help your body adjust, and it is not where most of the effect comes from. After four weeks the dose usually moves up in 2.5 mg steps to 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5 and up to 15 mg, with at least four weeks at each. Most people settle at 5 mg, 10 mg or 15 mg.

    What to Expect Week by Week in Peoria

    The first month is mostly quiet. You may notice a smaller appetite within the first week, and stomach upset tends to cluster in the day or two after a shot or a dose increase. In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, adults on the highest dose lost about 20 percent of body weight on average over 72 weeks. Individual results vary.

    Zepbound Side Effects

    Nausea and diarrhea come up most. Vomiting, constipation, indigestion, stomach pain and redness at the injection site are also common, especially during the first days after each dose increase. Serious but less common risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney injury from dehydration and severe allergic reactions. Zepbound carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening in Peoria

    Your provider starts with height, weight and BMI. Next comes your medical history and every medication you take, with special attention to insulin, sulfonylureas and birth control pills. Anyone with medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 in their family is not a candidate, and Zepbound is not used while pregnant. Labs may be requested.

    Zepbound Compared With Related Options in Peoria

    Zepbound and Mounjaro are the same medicine. The only real difference is the approved use, weight for Zepbound and type 2 diabetes for Mounjaro, which affects coverage and paperwork. A direct comparison trial found larger average weight loss with tirzepatide than with Wegovy. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved and is a different product from Zepbound.
